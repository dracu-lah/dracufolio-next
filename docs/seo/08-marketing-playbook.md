## Part D. Four offers that make people message

An offer is a reason to start a conversation today instead of bookmarking the site. Each one
below is honest, costs little, and can be withdrawn at any time.

### 1. Free 20 minute WhatsApp call for Thrissur district projects

- **What it is**: a 20 minute call where you say what you would build, roughly how long it takes
  and what it would cost. No sales pitch, no obligation.
- **Why it works**: a small business owner will not fill in a form but will send a WhatsApp
  message, and 20 minutes is a small enough ask to say yes to.
- **Where it goes**: `/hire`, the Business Profile description, the location pages, the WhatsApp
  greeting, the Google Posts.
- **The line to use**: `Free 20 minute call if your project is in Thrissur district. Tell me what you need on WhatsApp.`

### 2. Free speed and SEO check

- **What it is**: you run their site through PageSpeed Insights, screenshot the score, and send
  three specific fixes with what each one would change. 20 minutes of work.
- **Why it works**: it is proof of skill before any money changes hands, and the screenshot is
  hard to ignore. It also opens the door with agencies and list-post authors, see `05`.
- **Where it goes**: cold outreach, list-post outreach, LinkedIn posts, Google Posts.
- **The rule**: three fixes, no more. A twelve-item audit reads as a sales document and gets
  ignored.

### 3. Website in 14 days

- **What it is**: a small business website (up to five pages, mobile first, Google Business
  Profile set up, basic SEO and schema done) live in 14 days from the day content arrives.
- **Why it works**: a fixed timeline answers the question every small business owner actually
  has, which is "when".
- **Do not put a price on the page.** A published price gets compared to a 5,000 rupee template
  site and the conversation ends there. Price after scope, in a conversation. The reply for a
  price-only enquiry is in `10-outreach-templates.md`.
- **The honest caveat to say out loud**: the 14 days start when the content and photos arrive,
  not when the deposit does. Say it on the call, not after.

### 4. Referral thank-you

- **What it is**: anyone who refers a client that signs gets a real thank-you. A gift, a meal,
  or a discount on their own next project. Their choice.
- **Why it works**: referrals are the highest-converting channel and most people never ask.
- **The rule**: a referral thank-you is fine. Paying someone for a review, a testimonial or a
  link is not, see `03` and `05`.

## Part E. The proof to collect

Proof is what every audience in Part B asks for, in different words. Collect it deliberately.

| Proof | Target | How to get it | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google reviews | 5 in month 1, 15 by month 6 | `03`, Part B | The Business Profile, and the count quoted on `/hire` |
| Written testimonials | 3 real ones | Ask right after a successful delivery, template in `10` | `/hire`, LinkedIn recommendations, project pages |
| Real numbers on project pages | 1 per project, minimum | Measure before shipping. Load time, render time, users, records handled, cost saved | Project pages, blog posts, `llms-full.txt` |
| Screenshots | 3 per project | Real screens, no mockup frames, no fake data | Project pages, Business Profile photos, LinkedIn posts |
| Before and after PageSpeed | 2 | Keep the screenshots from the speed-fix offer | `/hire`, LinkedIn, Google Posts |
| Speaker pages | 2 | `05`, priority 4 | `/about`, the `sameAs` list |
| Play Store or release numbers | as they come | TMPlayer installs, GitHub stars, npm downloads | Project pages, X, the README |
| LinkedIn recommendations | 3 | Ask the same colleagues who left Google reviews, it is a different field and both count | LinkedIn profile |

Never invent a number, never round a number into something untrue, and never write a
testimonial for someone else to approve. One fabricated number found by a client costs more
than every number on the page is worth.

## Part F. The 30 minute monthly measurement routine

First Saturday of the month. Set a timer. The point is to notice a trend, not to produce a
report.

| Minutes | Where | What to write down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 8 | Search Console | Total impressions and clicks for the last 28 days. Position for `nevil krishna k`. The top 5 queries. Any new query with a place name in it. Indexed page count. |
| 8 to 14 | Google Business Profile > Performance | Searches that showed the profile, calls, website clicks, and the top 5 search terms people used. |
| 14 to 19 | Vercel Analytics | Visitors, top 5 pages, top referrers. Whether `/hire` is in the top 5 pages. |
| 19 to 27 | The AI check | The 6 queries from `06`, graded and logged. |
| 27 to 30 | The leads sheet | Total enquiries this month, how many became conversations, how many became money. One line on what caused the best one. |

### The leads sheet

One spreadsheet, one row per enquiry. Columns:

| Column | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Date | When they first made contact |
| Name | Person or business |
| Place | Town or city. This is the column that proves which location pages work |
| Channel | Google Maps, WhatsApp, site form, LinkedIn, referral, X, Upwork, JustDial, other |
| What they want | One line |
| Stage | New, talked, quoted, won, lost, ghosted |
| Quoted | Amount, if it got that far |
| Won | Amount, if it closed |
| Why lost | Price, timeline, went quiet, not a fit, hired someone else |
| Next action and date | The single most useful column. An enquiry with no next action is a lost one |

After three months this sheet answers the only question that matters: which channel brings work
that closes. Then double down on that one and stop spending time on the rest.
